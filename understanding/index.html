    Understanding the results

    maxodiff produces the same results page whether you start it from SAMS, from the web server, or from the command line. This page explains how to read it.

    The idea in one paragraph

    You start with a ranked list of candidate diseases (the differential diagnosis), where rank 1 is the most likely disease. For each diagnostic procedure, maxodiff simulates what the procedure might find and checks how much the list would change. Procedures that are expected to change the list more are ranked higher, because they are more likely to help you tell the candidates apart. See Background for the method.

    Page overview

    From top to bottom, the page shows:

    1. Summary of your input: the observed and excluded HPO terms, the number of diseases, and the number of simulations (repetitions).
    2. Detailed results for the 10 highest-scoring procedures.
    3. Other tests: a compact table for all remaining procedures.

    If none of the procedures is expected to change the ranking, the page says so instead of showing results.

    The score (ΔScore)

    Every procedure has one number, the ΔScore. It is used to order the page: the procedure with the largest ΔScore comes first.

    In each simulation, maxodiff compares the new ranking with the initial ranking. For every disease it takes the number of rank positions that the disease moved and divides it by the disease's initial rank. The sum over all diseases is the score of that simulation, and the ΔScore is the average over all simulations.

    Dividing by the initial rank means that a move near the top of the list counts more than the same move further down. Moving from rank 1 to rank 3 counts as 2, moving from rank 10 to rank 12 counts as 0.2.

    Note

    The ΔScore has no direction. It measures how much the list changes, not whether it gets better or worse. The direction for each disease is shown by the bars described below.

    The ΔScore is a relative measure for ordering procedures for one patient. Do not compare ΔScore values between different patients or between runs with different numbers of diseases.

    A detailed result

    Each of the top 10 procedures has its own box.

    Header

    • The MAxO term and its identifier (for example MAXO:0000973).
    • ΔScore, as described above.
    • N Diseases: the number of diseases that were ranked.
    • N Observed HPO Terms: the number of HPO terms that this procedure could reveal and that are not yet part of the patient's description.

    Average rank change per disease

    The bar chart shows one row per disease, in the order of the initial differential diagnosis (the initial rank is shown before the disease name).

    • The middle line means "no change".
    • A bar to the left (blue) means that the disease moves up the list on average, to a smaller rank number: it becomes more likely.
    • A bar to the right (red) means that the disease moves down the list on average: it becomes less likely.
    • One unit is one rank position. The scale at the top shows the values. The numbers are averages over all simulations, so they need not be whole numbers. Hover over a bar to see the exact change, for example "Decline by 2.0".

    What to look for

    A good procedure moves some candidate diseases clearly up and others clearly down. If all bars are short, the procedure is not expected to help distinguish between the candidates.

    Which findings could the procedure reveal?

    Under the chart, a table lists the HPO terms that the procedure could reveal (one column per term).

    • Observed in shows in how many of the simulations the term was drawn as a simulated finding. A darker cell means a more frequent term. Terms that are annotated to the highly ranked diseases are drawn more often. Hover over a cell to see how frequently the term is annotated to the candidate diseases.
    • Each row is a disease. The shading of a cell shows how strongly the term is associated with that disease, and the number is the information content of the most informative common ancestor (see below). An empty cell means that no such association was found.

    Other tests

    Below the top 10, one table lists all other procedures with:

    Column Meaning
    # Position of the procedure in the overall list
    MAxO term Name and identifier
    ΔScore The score described above
    HPO terms Number of HPO terms that the procedure could reveal
    Diseases moving up / down Number of diseases whose average rank gets smaller / larger

    For these procedures, the full details are available in the JSON output.

    Why results can differ slightly between runs

    The result of a procedure is simulated by drawing HPO terms at random. Two runs for the same patient can therefore give slightly different numbers, especially with few simulations. If the order of the top procedures changes between runs, increase the number of simulations. Large differences between the top procedures and the rest are stable.

    Limits of the method

    • The results are estimates, built on HPO annotations of diseases and MAxO annotations of procedures. If an annotation is missing, the procedure's value may be underestimated.
    • maxodiff does not consider cost, risk, invasiveness, or availability of a procedure. These are clinical decisions.
    • The analysis is limited to the diseases of the differential diagnosis. The number of diseases (20 to 40) and the number of simulations (1 to 100) can be chosen.
